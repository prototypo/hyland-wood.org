  • slavery · Mesopotamia (Sumer and Babylon) · 2100 BCE – 1750 BCE
    The years are not exact.
    Point: 45.8, 31.3
    Note: The oldest known code of laws, written about 2100 to 2050 BCE in Sumer, has laws about slaves. The Code of Hammurabi of Babylon, of about 1750 BCE, also tells the free from the enslaved.

    The years are not exact.
    Point: 31.2, 26.5
    Note: There was slavery in ancient Egypt at least from the Old Kingdom. The first record is from the reign of King Sneferu, in the 26th century BCE, about captives from wars in Nubia. The article follows slavery to the Ptolemaic Kingdom and to Roman Egypt.
    Source
  • slavery · Ancient Greece
    Areas of the map: Greek City-States, Second Athenian League, Athenian Coalition
    Note: Slavery was a widely accepted practice in ancient Greece. Most slaves worked on farms; others worked in quarries, in mines and in homes. The records are in pieces, and most of them are about Athens.
    Source
  • slavery · Ancient Rome
    Areas of the map: Roman Republic, Roman Empire, Western Roman Empire, Eastern Roman Empire
    Note: Slavery had an important part in the society and the economy of ancient Rome. By Roman law, slaves were property. Rome's oldest code of laws, of about 450 BCE, takes slavery as a given. In the years 260 to 425 CE, 10 to 15% of the people of the empire were slaves.
    Source
  • slavery · Byzantine Empire
    Areas of the map: Byzantine Empire
    Note: Slavery had a notable part in the economy of the Byzantine Empire. Many slaves came from wars. With time, laws gave slaves more rights, and slavery lost a part of its weight in the economy.
